# Buyer Access Activation Plan: Founder-Led Renewal Interviews

Last updated: 2026-08-07
Status: Active activation artifact
Task ID: TASK-007

## Purpose

This plan converts the buyer-access blocker from `TASK-006` into a practical founder-led outreach system.

The goal is not to automate customer access.

The goal is to help the founder book the first real interviews quickly, with the right people, using the right explanation.

## Success Target

The near-term target is:

- `12` interviews
- across at least `8` companies
- with at least `4` interviewees open to a live or recent renewal teardown if the pain is real

Working assumption:

- expect to source roughly `40` to `60` qualified contacts to land the first `12` conversations
- bias toward warm and second-degree paths before cold outreach

This volume assumption is an Atlas operating estimate, not a validated benchmark.

## Who To Contact First

Prioritize people who actually feel renewal pain without sitting inside a mature procurement function.

### Priority 1 Roles

- VP Finance
- Finance Director or Controller
- Head of Operations
- COO at smaller mid-market companies
- IT Director or Head of IT
- systems owner who inherits renewals without a sourcing team

Why these first:

- they often own budget pressure
- they see late notice windows and unused spend
- they can describe the real tradeoff between renew, renegotiate, downgrade, or replace

### Priority 2 Roles

- BizOps leader
- RevOps or internal operations lead when they coordinate tooling decisions
- procurement-adjacent operator without a formal procurement title

### Lower Priority Roles

- dedicated enterprise procurement leaders
- junior tool admins without decision visibility
- founders of tiny companies with too little software spend

These are lower priority because they either have too much process, too little decision context, or too little renewal volume for the wedge.

## Which Companies To Target

Use this filter:

- `150` to `1,000` employees
- meaningful SaaS and AI spend
- no large strategic sourcing or procurement team
- likely multiple renewals in the next `120` days
- enough operational complexity that tool sprawl and renewal timing matter

## Best Target Archetypes

Start with these company shapes:

1. PE-backed service or software-enabled businesses with cost pressure and lean central teams.
2. Multi-site operators where software spend is fragmented across functions or locations.
3. Mid-market B2B SaaS companies that run many internal tools but do not have a heavy procurement layer.
4. Tech-enabled business services firms experimenting with AI tools on top of an already crowded SaaS stack.
5. Growth-stage companies where finance, ops, and IT share renewal responsibility informally.

Avoid early:

- Fortune 500 companies with formal sourcing teams
- very small startups with fewer than a handful of meaningful renewals
- companies whose buyers are unlikely to discuss process without heavy compliance steps

## Best Channels In Order

Use channels in this order:

1. Warm intros from your own network
2. Second-degree intros through founders, operators, investors, or advisors
3. Direct outreach to known relevant operators
4. Cold LinkedIn or email to fill the remaining gaps
5. Referral asks after every good interview

### Warm Intro Sources

Start with people who can route you to the right operator:

- former colleagues
- founder friends
- angel or investor relationships
- advisors
- finance leaders you already know
- IT or ops operators in your network
- people who have recently lived through software cost-cutting or AI-tool sprawl

### Second-Degree Intro Sources

Ask each helpful contact:

- who owns software renewals when there is no procurement team?
- who feels the pain when notice windows are missed or tools are underused?
- who has a live renewal coming up in the next quarter?

### Cold Outreach Use Case

Only use cold outreach after you have tightened the talk track through warm conversations.

Cold outreach should target narrow role-plus-company combinations rather than broad blasting.

## What To Say You Are Doing

Use this plain-English explanation:

`I'm doing founder-led research on how mid-market companies handle SaaS and AI renewals when there isn't a dedicated procurement team. I'm not selling software in this conversation. I'm trying to understand how renewal decisions actually happen, where teams lose leverage, and whether benchmark-backed decision support would materially help before renewal deadlines lock the outcome.`

## Short Explanation

Use this when someone asks in one sentence:

`I'm researching how lean mid-market teams decide whether to renew, renegotiate, downgrade, or replace software and AI vendors when no one owns procurement full-time.`

## If They Ask What You Are Building

Do not hide the direction, but do not pitch a finished product.

Use:

`I have a hypothesis around benchmark-backed renewal decision support, but right now I'm much more interested in understanding the real workflow, failure points, and economics than in selling a fixed solution.`

## The Interview Ask

Use this ask:

- `20` minutes
- focused on a recent or upcoming renewal workflow
- no sales pitch
- optional follow-up only if the first conversation is relevant

Ask for a teardown only after the pain is confirmed in the interview.

Do not lead with the teardown request.

## Founder Operating Sequence

### Step 1: Build the first target pool

Create a first pass of `30` to `40` companies split across:

- `10` to `15` warm or near-warm targets
- `10` to `15` second-degree targets
- `10` cold targets reserved for coverage gaps

### Step 2: Prioritize by pain, not prestige

Rank targets by:

1. renewal pain likelihood
2. access likelihood
3. role relevance
4. company fit

### Step 3: Send the first batch fast

Send:

- the first `10` warm messages immediately
- the next `10` second-degree requests within the same cycle
- cold messages only after the first message wording feels crisp

### Step 4: Track every touch

For every target, log:

- channel
- warm path
- last touch date
- reply status
- next action

### Step 5: Use every interview to open the next one

At the end of every useful interview, ask:

- who else on your side feels this pain?
- who at another company handles this well or badly?
- is there one renewal you would be comfortable revisiting at a high level?

## Cadence

Use a tight founder cadence:

1. source and rank targets
2. send outreach the same day
3. follow up `4` to `5` days later
4. schedule interviews in clusters of `3` to `5`
5. debrief immediately after each call
6. ask for referrals before closing every strong conversation

## What Good Early Progress Looks Like

The first success signs are:

- warm contacts can name the right operator quickly
- interviewees immediately recognize late notice, shelfware, pricing pressure, or tool overlap as real pain
- at least some interviewees are willing to discuss a recent renewal in detail
- at least `4` of the first `12` interviewees are open to a teardown follow-up

## What Would Worry Us Early

Warning signs:

- everyone routes you to formal procurement teams at large enterprises
- lean mid-market operators say renewals are trivial and not worth attention
- interviewees cannot recall meaningful renewal mistakes or leverage gaps
- nobody is willing to revisit even a sanitized recent renewal
