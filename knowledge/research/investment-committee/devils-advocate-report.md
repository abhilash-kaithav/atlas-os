# Devil's Advocate Report

Last updated: 2026-08-16
Status: Complete

## W-001

- Why won't customers buy?
  - Some subcontractors may accept the pain as controller labor rather than new software spend.
- Why will incumbents solve this?
  - Portal and construction-fintech vendors could improve rejection visibility and waiver-state diagnosis.
- Why is the market too small?
  - If only a narrow slice of subcontractors have recurring multi-portal rejection pain, the product may cap out quickly.
- Why is implementation unrealistic?
  - Document states, portal behaviors, and GC-specific rules may prove too bespoke.
- Why is software inferior to services?
  - Experienced project accountants may still be faster on unusual disputes than a product can be.
- Why is AI insufficient?
  - If the problem is missing authority or negotiation rather than diagnosis, better extraction will not solve it.
- Most fragile assumption:
  - Enough rejected pay apps share recurring blocker types to make the workflow productizable.
- Evidence that would invalidate the thesis immediately:
  - Pilot users report that each rejected pay app is effectively bespoke and that portal vendors already expose the needed blocker state.
- Committee conclusion:
  - Survives the adversarial review because none of these objections currently outweigh the strength of the evidence and economics.

## W-002

- Why won't customers buy?
  - Operators may tolerate the problem with coordinators, vendor relationships, and PMS workarounds.
- Why will incumbents solve this?
  - PMS and maintenance vendors can plausibly add blocker visibility and turn boards.
- Why is the market too small?
  - If the wedge remains limited to turns rather than broader readiness recovery, it may not support the company case.
- Why is implementation unrealistic?
  - A large portion of missed ready dates may come from capex backlog, labor shortages, or vendor quality, not information failure.
- Why is software inferior to services?
  - Human coordinators may remain the cheapest way to clear messy vendor and access blockers.
- Why is AI insufficient?
  - Prediction and summarization do not help if the real delay is budget approval or resource scarcity.
- Most fragile assumption:
  - Missed ready dates are primarily coordination failures that software can materially compress.
- Evidence that would invalidate the thesis immediately:
  - Operators show that most delays come from structural capex or staffing scarcity and that PMS add-ons already cover the software-addressable portion.
- Committee conclusion:
  - Survives, but not strongly enough for `Tier 1`. This report is the main reason the candidate lands in `Tier 2`.
