## Current-State Mapping

- Objective: Preserve profitable relationships by identifying risk early and executing the right renewal, retention, or remarketing action.
- Trigger: A contract, account, subscriber, or customer relationship shows renewal timing or churn risk.
- End outcome: The relationship is renewed, recovered, remarketed, or exited with a clear ownership path.
- Primary actors: account owner; customer success or retention team; customer; commercial approver
- Major decisions: Is the relationship healthy enough to renew as is?; What price, package, or intervention is worth offering?; When should the account be saved, reshaped, or allowed to churn?
- Major handoffs: usage or performance signals -> account owner; retention motion -> pricing or finance review; renewed account -> delivery, support, or billing team
- Systems of record involved: Loan Origination and Servicing | Maintenance Management | Dealership Management System | CRM

## Current-State Friction

- Where money is lost: Late risk detection, weak expansion discipline, and reactive renewal handling erode retention economics.
- Where time is lost: Teams assemble context manually across service records, invoices, and relationship notes before every renewal conversation.
- Where human judgment dominates: Renewal strategy depends on relationship strength, future potential, and nuanced commercial tradeoffs.
- Where people leave the system of record: The decisive conversations happen in calls, emails, and side planning decks outside the system of record.
