## Current-State Mapping

- Objective: Deliver scheduled instruction or learning services while keeping attendance, progress, and support needs aligned.
- Trigger: A course, lesson, or instructional block reaches delivery time.
- End outcome: Instruction is delivered, participation is recorded, and follow-up actions are visible for students and staff.
- Primary actors: instructor; student; academic or program coordinator; support staff
- Major decisions: How should pacing or support be adjusted for current student needs?; What absence, issue, or escalation requires intervention?; What evidence is required to confirm participation or completion?
- Major handoffs: enrollment and scheduling -> instructional staff; instruction -> student support or administration; course outcomes -> billing, reporting, or retention workflows
- Systems of record involved: Learning Management System | Student Information System | ERP | HCM / Workforce Management

## Current-State Friction

- Where money is lost: Leakage appears through attrition, underfilled capacity, compliance misses, and avoidable remediation cost.
- Where time is lost: Faculty and administrators spend time on attendance tracking, follow-up, and reconciling systems with actual class activity.
- Where human judgment dominates: Instruction quality and intervention timing still depend on educator judgment.
- Where people leave the system of record: Critical context lives in conversations, notes, side messages, and informal support channels outside SIS and LMS records.

## Software Landscape

- What software exists today: Typical stacks combine Learning Management System, Student Information System, ERP, HCM / Workforce Management; representative software in market today includes Canvas LMS, D2L Brightspace, Blackboard, Ellucian Student, PowerSchool, Workday Student.
- Representative vendors: Canvas LMS; D2L Brightspace; Blackboard; Ellucian Student; PowerSchool; Workday Student; SAP Cloud ERP; Acumatica Cloud ERP
- Why this has not been solved cleanly: Learning delivery is partly standardized, but student variance and faculty autonomy keep the workflow human-led. It typically spans 1 operating-system context and 4 systems-of-record categories.
- Primary reason: `Behavioral`
