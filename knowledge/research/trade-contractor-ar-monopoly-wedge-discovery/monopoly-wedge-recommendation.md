# Monopoly Wedge Recommendation

Last updated: 2026-08-14
Status: Final recommendation

## Recommendation

Preserve exactly one wedge:

> subcontractor-side pay-application rejection diagnosis and resubmission orchestration

Kill the broader commercial trade-contractor AR category as a company boundary.

## Exact Workflow Boundary

The wedge starts when a submitted or draft-complete pay app is blocked by a GC-specific requirement or inconsistency.

Examples:

- the SOV does not match what the GC expects,
- an approved change order has not been added correctly,
- the waiver packet is wrong, missing, or mistimed,
- compliance is expired,
- a required form, backup, or portal rule changed,
- the packet status is unclear and the team does not know what exactly to correct.

The wedge ends when the corrected packet is resubmitted and the blocker is cleared or explicitly escalated.

## Why This Is The Best Beachhead

- It sits at the highest-frequency cash choke point.
- It is narrow enough to implement without replacing ERP, PM, or portal software.
- It is buyer-visible to one team: controller, AR lead, billing manager.
- It expands naturally:
  - upstream into SOV setup and first-pass packet creation,
  - downstream into aging, collections, retainage, and cash forecasting.

## Why Incumbents Still Leave Room

- Procore and Autodesk optimize the GC or payor-controlled flow.
- Sage and Vista optimize accounting record-keeping and job-cost mechanics.
- Direct sub-AR startups are emerging, but most market themselves as broader billing or AR platforms rather than a dedicated exception desk.

## Realistic Entry Point

### First ICP

- Commercial specialty contractors
- Multiple active GC relationships
- High project count or high monthly pay-app volume
- Existing ERP already in place
- Existing pain around missed cutoffs, rejected draws, and opaque portal rules

### First job to be done

"When our pay app gets blocked, tell us exactly what failed, what to fix, and help us get the corrected packet back in before we lose the month."

## What Must Be Validated Next

1. Do controllers and AR managers view rejected or held pay apps as a standalone budget-worthy problem?
2. How often does a rejection actually push cash into the next cycle?
3. Which blockers are most common by GC and portal type?
4. Would buyers adopt a neutral exception layer without asking their GC to change systems?

If those answers are weak, kill the wedge too.
