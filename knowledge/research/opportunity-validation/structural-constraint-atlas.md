## SF-01 Exception-Path Breakdown

- Root cause or symptom: Symptom cluster with concentrated economics
- Dominant constraint: Legacy Architecture
- Constraint strength: Medium-High
- Why it persists: Incumbents automate the happy path but leave fragmented data, policy nuance, and local recovery work to humans once the flow deviates.
- Recurrence: 77 workflows, 99 usage links, 20 operating systems, 48 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $44.03T gross output; $25.50T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Finance and Revenue Operations (15); Delivery and Service Execution (7); Access, Intake, and Contracting (6).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (22); CRM (11); Maintenance Management (8); Supply Chain Planning (7).
- Phase 4 verdict: Advance as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: Exception work is not the deepest root cause, but it is where cash leakage and service failure are concentrated, which makes it venture-relevant.

## SF-02 Cross-System Reconciliation

- Root cause or symptom: Root cause
- Dominant constraint: Legacy Architecture
- Constraint strength: High
- Why it persists: Authoritative truth is distributed across asynchronous systems and counterparties, so reconciliation remains a manual control layer rather than a solved background task.
- Recurrence: 42 workflows, 47 usage links, 17 operating systems, 35 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $34.14T gross output; $20.48T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Finance and Revenue Operations (11); Governance and Portfolio Operations (6).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: CRM (9); Investor Reporting and Performance (6); Portfolio and Order Management (6); Fund Administration and Accounting (5).
- Phase 4 verdict: Advance as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: The pressure is less universal than SF-03, but the workflows are economically dense and timing has improved because reconciliation windows are tightening.

## SF-03 Decision Context Escapes the Record

- Root cause or symptom: Root cause
- Dominant constraint: Legacy Architecture
- Constraint strength: High
- Why it persists: Structured systems capture state changes, but decisive narrative context stays in collaboration channels, attachments, and portals.
- Recurrence: 174 workflows, 219 usage links, 21 operating systems, 50 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $45.42T gross output; $26.31T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Access, Intake, and Contracting (9); Product, Content, and Engineering (9); Finance and Revenue Operations (8).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: CRM (30); ERP (26); HCM / Workforce Management (16); Service Management (11).
- Phase 4 verdict: Advance as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: This is Atlas's strongest cross-industry invariant and the best candidate for a new system layer rather than another point tool.

## SF-04 Human Judgment Under Incomplete Information

- Root cause or symptom: Symptom-leaning cross-cutting condition
- Dominant constraint: Technical
- Constraint strength: Medium-High
- Why it persists: Judgment remains human because the underlying context is incomplete, noisy, and politically negotiated rather than because no dashboard exists.
- Recurrence: 116 workflows, 149 usage links, 21 operating systems, 50 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $45.42T gross output; $26.31T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Planning and Allocation (14); Customer and Experience Operations (8); Production and Asset Operations (6).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (25); CRM (13); Supply Chain Planning (10); Maintenance Management (9).
- Phase 4 verdict: Reject as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: A generic AI judgment copilot is too horizontal and too easy for incumbents to add as a feature unless it is anchored in proprietary context capture or a closed-loop workflow system.

## SF-05 Handoff and Approval Latency

- Root cause or symptom: Mostly symptom of coordination design
- Dominant constraint: Organizational
- Constraint strength: High
- Why it persists: The hard part is not task automation but changing accountability, approvals, and cross-functional behavior.
- Recurrence: 40 workflows, 46 usage links, 18 operating systems, 32 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $34.55T gross output; $20.91T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Access, Intake, and Contracting (8); Governance and Portfolio Operations (7).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (10); Service Management (7); CRM (6); HCM / Workforce Management (6).
- Phase 4 verdict: Reject as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: The category is crowded with BPM, onboarding, and ticketing software, while the constraint itself is often political rather than technical.

## SF-06 Plan vs. Reality Divergence

- Root cause or symptom: Root cause inside dynamic physical operations
- Dominant constraint: Legacy Architecture + Technical
- Constraint strength: High
- Why it persists: Plans decay faster than incumbent planning suites can absorb new field conditions, partner data, and changing local constraints.
- Recurrence: 53 workflows, 76 usage links, 13 operating systems, 35 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $29.89T gross output; $16.74T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Production and Asset Operations (9); Network and Transportation Operations (8); Planning and Allocation (3).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (18); Maintenance Management (6); Supply Chain Planning (6); Manufacturing Execution System (4).
- Phase 4 verdict: Advance as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: This is the cleanest path to a live control layer above existing planning and execution software in logistics, field, and industrial operations.

- Root cause or symptom: Root cause in regulated domains
- Dominant constraint: Regulatory
- Constraint strength: High
- Why it persists: Reporting forms can be standardized, but evidence lineage, proof collection, and materiality judgments still cut across people, documents, and external systems.
- Recurrence: 29 workflows, 34 usage links, 14 operating systems, 23 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $17.11T gross output; $9.18T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Risk, Compliance, and Reporting (17); Finance and Revenue Operations (2).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (10); CRM (8); Manufacturing Execution System (6); Industrial Automation and SCADA (4).
- Phase 4 verdict: Advance as standalone thesis.
- Commentary: This is narrower than SF-03, but the combination of regulatory timing and evidence collection pain creates a believable entry wedge.

## SF-08 Multi-Party Trust and Dependency Gaps

- Root cause or symptom: Root cause with adoption-friction ceiling
- Dominant constraint: Network Effects + Market Fragmentation
- Constraint strength: Very high
- Why it persists: The workflow fails at enterprise boundaries, but the same boundary also makes first deployment, data standardization, and network adoption slow.
- Recurrence: 28 workflows, 39 usage links, 12 operating systems, 27 industries.
- Market surface in Atlas: $20.01T gross output; $10.05T value added.
- Top workflow families where it is primary: Sourcing and Supply (7).
- Typical system categories when it is primary: ERP (5); Supply Chain Planning (3); PLM and Engineering Design (3); Maintenance Management (2).
- Phase 4 verdict: Reject as first-company thesis.
- Commentary: The opportunity may become attractive later, but as an initial wedge it asks a startup to solve trust, integration, and adoption on both sides of the network at once.
