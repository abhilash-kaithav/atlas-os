## Wedge Notes

### 1. Trade-contractor pay-app exceptions

- Buyer: controller, CFO, head of AR, or billing manager at a commercial trade contractor.
- Current budget footprint:
  - accounting and ERP stack,
  - staff time for billing coordinators and AR,
  - mandatory participation in owner or GC-side portals.
- Cost of failure:
  - delayed payment,
  - rejected or revised pay apps,
  - compliance misses,
  - extra follow-up cycles with PMs, supers, GCs, and accounting.
- Why net-new software is believable:
  - the trade contractor still owns the receivable risk,
  - upstream portals are not built to optimize the trade contractor's workflow,
  - cash-flow visibility is direct and operationally painful.

### 2. Provider denials and recoupments

- Buyer: clear and valuable.
- Cost of failure: very high.
- Why it still lost:
  - the market is now too occupied.
  - Waystar's recoupment product alone shows that even "silent denials" are already being directly monetized by a large incumbent.

### 3. SaaS monetization exceptions

- Buyer: finance and revenue operations leaders.
- Cost of failure:
  - incorrect invoices,
  - poor customer trust,
  - delayed pricing launches,
  - manual reconciliations and credits.
- Why it still lost:
  - this pain is now visible enough that both traditional vendors and API-native vendors are heavily investing.

## Economics Conclusion

The problem was not finding workflows with money attached.

The problem was finding a workflow where:

1. the buyer already spends money,
2. the buyer still feels acute pain,
3. and the current vendor set still leaves a narrow opening.

Only the construction trade-contractor wedge currently clears all three tests.
