# Buyer Economics Report

Last updated: 2026-08-14
Status: Active Phase 4B economics layer

## Buyer Economics Matrix

| Candidate | Economic buyer | Measurable pain | WTP / ROI drivers | Economics verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OV-01 | Ambiguous across CMIO, ops, revenue-cycle, and platform owners | Documentation drift, rework, lost rationale, slower escalations | Benefits are real but often indirect and bundled into larger workflow or platform budgets | Weak as a standalone buying center |
| OV-02 | CFO, VP Revenue Cycle, VP Managed Care, denials leader | AHA estimates hospitals spent $43B in 2025 collecting insurer payments already owed, with nearly $18B tied to overturning denials | Faster cash, recovered revenue, fewer appeal hours, reduced write-offs, recoupment recovery | Strong economics, weak defensibility |
| OV-03 | Controller, CAO, shared-services lead, transportation-finance lead | Overcharges, delayed close, manual breaks, carrier-billing leakage | Savings recovery, faster close, audit control, working-capital visibility | Strong economics, occupied category |
| OV-04 | COO, supply-chain leader, turnaround leader, logistics leader | Expediting, idle crews, delay costs, service failures, disruption overhead | Cost avoidance, service protection, higher utilization, lower manual coordination | Strong economics, but leaders already target the wedge |
| OV-05 | Chief Compliance Officer, quality leader, audit leader, oversight leader | Repeated evidence chase, audit prep labor, delayed remediation, finding risk | Labor reduction, faster audits, fewer findings, better readiness, avoided penalties | Real budget, but current vendors already sell directly into it |

## Notes By Candidate

### OV-01

This was the weakest standalone budget story.

The pain is usually recognized by operators, but the purchase still tends to depend on another system owner already holding the workflow budget. That makes the thesis highly exposed to bundling by collaboration, EHR, CRM, and workflow incumbents.

### OV-02

This was the clearest pain-to-budget path.

The AHA's 2026 data and CMS's current prior-authorization regime both support a large, measurable administrative burden. That said, strong economics do not rescue the thesis once direct category leaders are already attacking the same wedge.

### OV-03

The market clearly spends money here.

Freight-audit vendors publicize overcharge recovery and transport-cost control. Finance-reconciliation vendors publicize close acceleration, match-rate improvement, and audit-readiness. Atlas therefore found budget density, but not wedge vacancy.

### OV-04

The economics are compelling wherever schedule slip is expensive.

Examples include capital projects, industrial turnarounds, and network logistics. The problem is that current control-tower and execution vendors already understand those economics and are positioning into them directly.

### OV-05

Compliance budgets exist when the audit pressure is real.

The wedge did not fail because buyers lack money. It failed because the software landscape already reflects that budget and includes both horizontal and vertical specialists.

## Bottom Line

Phase 4B did not expose a buyer-economics problem.

It exposed a defensibility problem.
