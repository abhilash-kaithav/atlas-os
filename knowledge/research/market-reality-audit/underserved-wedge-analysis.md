# Underserved Wedge Analysis

Last updated: 2026-08-14
Status: Active Phase 4B wedge layer

## Wedge Test Summary

| Candidate | Specific wedge tested | Why the wedge still exists | Why the wedge still does not earn GREEN |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OV-01 | Regulated clinical and claims-adjacent rationale capture | Narrative context still escapes structured systems and must be defended later. | The strongest entry points are already controlled by ambient documentation vendors, collaboration vendors, and workflow platforms. |
| OV-02 | Provider-side denials appeals and post-payment recoupment recovery | Payer-specific evidence assembly remains manual and expensive. | The exact pain surface is already being productized by Waystar-class vendors with data, payer-form, and workflow advantages. |
| OV-03 | Freight invoice audit and contract-rate reconciliation | Carrier billing remains fragmented, accessorial-heavy, and data-messy. | Direct freight-audit vendors already market the same recovery wedge with measurable ROI and long operating history. |
| OV-04 | Capital-project and industrial-turnaround recovery control | Procurement, shipment, and schedule signals still break apart under disruption. | FourKites, project44, and adjacent industrial operations vendors are already expanding into this precise recovery surface. |
| OV-05 | Medicare Advantage delegation-oversight evidence workflows | CMS audit pressure and external-delegate evidence collection remain ugly. | This is already a named vertical software category with purpose-built vendors, not a hidden whitespace. |

## Candidate Commentary

### OV-01

The wedge is real but too exposed.

The distribution points that matter most are the EHR, collaboration suite, CRM, and workflow platform. Atlas does not currently show a reason a startup would own those surfaces before incumbent platforms or vertical note vendors do.

### OV-02

This was the strongest near-miss in terms of buyer pain.

However, the evidence says Atlas discovered a large and attractive market, not an underexploited wedge. The current leaders are already turning denial appeals, payer follow-up, and even recoupments into AI-assisted operational workflows.

### OV-03

The wedge is economically dense but already named.

Freight audit and payment, intercompany reconciliation, and AI close operations are all current product categories with direct leaders. Atlas did not isolate a sub-wedge whose economics were strong while vendor occupancy remained weak.

### OV-04

The wedge remains operationally painful but not strategically underowned.

The remaining gap is a fragmented cross-domain problem rather than a clean category vacuum. That is an implementation challenge, not yet a clear startup boundary.

### OV-05

This wedge most clearly illustrates the Phase 4B lesson.

Generic GRC is not enough for many regulated workflows. But when Atlas narrowed into a real operational niche, it found vertical software vendors already built around that exact niche.

## Decision

No wedge passed the standard:

> materially underserved, narrow enough to enter, and still defensible if current category leaders focus on it.
