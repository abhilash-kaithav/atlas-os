# O-001 Compounding Behaviors

Last updated: 2026-08-03
Status: Active observation
Category: Behavior design

## Observation

Across five independent behavior batches, many of the strongest ideas create compounding loops rather than one-time automation.

## What Was Observed

- Thinking: `Cognitive Compounder` (#410) improves reasoning over time instead of offering a one-off answer.
- Remembering: `Memory Dividend` (#411) and `Human Pattern Archive` (#414) turn past experience into future leverage.
- Deciding: `Decision Compounder` (#430) makes every decision improve the next one.
- Learning: `Learning Loops` (#431) converts learning into a repeated learn-apply-reflect cycle.
- Relationships: `Relationship Flywheel` (#450) makes each interaction improve future interactions.

## Boundaries

- This note records a recurring pattern, not a conclusion that compounding loops always win.
- Counterexamples should be recorded when a top idea succeeds because of immediacy, utility, or distribution without a compounding loop.

## Review Implication

When evaluating future opportunities, explicitly ask whether repeated use creates increasing value or whether the concept only automates an isolated task.
